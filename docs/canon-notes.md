# Opening chapter: canon and adaptation notes

Research checked 17 October 2026. This is an implementation reference, not dialogue copied from the novel. Only the opening chapter is in scope.

## Author / publisher confirmation

- Jason wakes on a school bus without his memories; Piper and Leo believe they already know him. They attend the Wilderness School. Piper is worried about her missing father, but the opening should keep that thread understated. [Rick Riordan: The Lost Hero](https://rickriordan.com/book/the-lost-hero/)
- The field trip is to the Grand Canyon. Coach Hedge is their chaperone. Dylan is a disguised storm spirit. Jason's gold coin becomes a gold sword; Jason discovers flight. Annabeth later arrives in a flying chariot while searching for Percy. [Disney's Read Riordan book overview](https://www.readriordan.com/book/the-lost-hero/)
- Jason is a son of Jupiter. His coin can become a sword or javelin, but this chapter deliberately uses the sword. [Read Riordan: Jason Grace](https://www.readriordan.com/2018/04/12/spotlight-on-jason-grace/)
- Weapon audit, 18 October: Jason carries one golden sword in this encounter. The old idle sprite incorrectly included a second blade in his raised hand. Replaced that pose with `jason-idle-v2.png`, showing one sword and an empty fist; reviewed all four walk frames plus jump, block, strike and heavy poses for a single weapon. The original atlas remains a source for the other poses, but Jason's idle no longer uses its incorrect frame.
- Piper has charmspeak before attending the Wilderness School, although that does not mean she consciously understands it during the canyon encounter. Her voice can support a brief stagger mechanic without presenting her as an experienced spellcaster. [Read Riordan: Piper McLean](https://www.readriordan.com/2018/03/15/5-reasons-you-love-piper-mclean/)

## Detailed opening sequence

The following details are checked against the opening two chapters reproduced as a book-preview excerpt by Fresh Fiction, with publisher and author attribution. This is a secondary host of the primary text, not a publisher-owned URL. [Opening excerpt](https://www.freshfiction.com/excerpt.php?id=41817)

1. Museum exhibits precede the outdoor glass skywalk; the attack happens on the skywalk.
2. Hedge already watches Piper and Leo. Jason is an unexpected third demigod; Hedge warns him while awaiting help.
3. Leo has tampered with Hedge's megaphone on the bus. No specific unidentified Hedge quote should be called canonical.
4. Dylan throws Leo from the skywalk. Hedge reveals his hooves and climbs to rescue him.
5. Piper picks up Hedge's club. Jason survives Dylan's lightning before transforming his coin and destroying two accompanying spirits.
6. Piper tackles Dylan, then is thrown over the railing. Dylan tries to abduct Leo; Hedge frees Leo and is carried away instead.
7. Jason jumps after Piper, catches her, discovers flight, and brings her back.
8. The surviving trio question what happened. Jason still cannot explain his identity.

## Deliberate choices from Rodrigo's game brief

- Retain Rodrigo's visual designs, including the specific outfits and hair in the supplied lineup.
- Retain a museum fighting lane with the skywalk visible beyond it. The relocation is an adaptation, not the novel's exact staging.
- Retain the single smoky wind opponent and the explicit prohibition on electrical enemy effects. Do not add the novel's lightning blast merely to reproduce its chronology.
- Retain all three playable heroes. Jason's coin transformation and rescue remain in the story regardless of the selected fighter.
- Keep Piper's opening weapon a borrowed club if possible. Do not describe Katoptris as an established possession during this encounter; later possession is not evidence for opening-chapter possession.
- Prefer Leo's tools and improvised gadgets here. A confident public fire moveset would be a gameplay adaptation. The sources checked establish his mechanical skill, but do not verify the precise later point at which he reveals fire to his friends; do not claim a chapter number without further checking.
- Show the enemy escaping with Hedge after the player wins the encounter. Victory means protecting the trio, not permanently destroying Dylan.
- The four prophecy lines and their timing are user-supplied requirements. Use that text exactly as requested; presenting it before the bus is deliberate foreshadowing rather than the novel's scene order.
- Avoid later-series revelations in dialogue, including explaining the memory swap or naming the hidden enemy too early.
